About me

Gokarna Bahadur Thapa

Project Coordinator (BEACON), Prakriti Resources Centre

I’m a Mechanical Engineering graduate from Kathmandu University with professional experience in renewable energy, energy planning and climate policy. I work at the intersection of energy, climate policy and sustainable development.

I’m particularly interested in how access to clean and reliable energy can build stronger, more resilient communities. In my free time, I enjoy playing badminton, reading books and listening to music.

Projects

Selected work

Feasibility studies, energy models and policy support for Nepal’s government agencies and development partners.

Climate & energy

BEACON: community and national energy resilience

Coordinating a project funded by Bread for the World that links recommendations for 100% renewable energy in Nepal’s NDC 3.0 with a community energy transition plan in Melamchi Municipality.

Prakriti Resources Centre · 2026–
Waste-to-energy

Bio-CNG feasibility for six municipalities

Led the winning technical proposal, then designed 10–50 TPD anaerobic-digestion plants producing about 4.7 t/day of Bio-CNG to replace LPG, with IRR/NPV and IPCC-based GHG models.

AEPC · World Bank MGEAP · GCF pipeline
Green hydrogen

Stock-taking of green hydrogen in Nepal

Contributed technical inputs across fertiliser, industry and transport, and modelled 100 MW hydrogen and 150 MW green-urea plants: 14,048 t H₂ and 211,680 t urea per year.

AEPC
Energy modelling

Energy efficiency action plan 2026–2035

Built a LEAP demand model for Nepal’s residential, commercial, industrial and transport sectors to project demand and quantify savings under efficiency and electrification scenarios.

AEPC · LEAP
Climate policy

National carbon pricing policy

Supported analysis of carbon tax, ETS and hybrid options across energy, IPPU, waste and AFOLU, including a USD 1/tCO₂e tax projected to raise NPR 1.48 billion a year.

UNDP
Research

Second-life EV batteries for surplus hydropower

Assessed how used electric-vehicle batteries could store Nepal’s surplus hydropower. Published in the Journal of Energy Storage.
